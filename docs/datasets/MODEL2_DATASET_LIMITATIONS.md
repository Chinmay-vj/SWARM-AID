# Pilot limitations and readiness

All native records are from deterministic symbolic sensors, mock perception, an in-process router, shared-information survey assignment and rule-based command assessment. No camera pixels, thermal arrays, audio clips or synchronized physical sensors were generated. Cyclone and landslide reuse underlying disaster dynamics. The simulator lacks a dedicated missing-person mission. No rescue outcome or independently verified urgency judgment is available.

The 13 episodes demonstrate traceable handling of known conditions. They do not establish real-world reliability, calibration, source independence or user workload. Weak labels partly reproduce the same rules a learned model would be compared against. Stale and incomplete evidence require abstention. The pilot has no reviewed labels, one artificial identity conflict and no human confirmation, correction, resolution or closure targets.

Splits isolate missions, episodes and derived variants. A supplementary comparison stripped IDs and used finding kind/label, cell, confidence, sorted modality tags and report age for the focal report and ordered context. There were 374 distinct such group patterns; 38 occurred in more than one partition. At focal-report level 27 of 122 patterns crossed partitions. These are repeated symbolic patterns, not additional independent captures. Report performance with and without recurring patterns, hold out scenario families and compare against a pattern-lookup baseline before making generalization claims.

The local CrisisMMD v2.0 inventory covers seven public disaster events. Its labels describe social-media content. Independent rights and privacy review and a leakage-safe public partition are pending. Phase 10B–D public holdings have separate review gates. Provider class names, Git revision, dirty state and selected source/config digests are recorded. An independently versioned provider package, complete immutable source checkout and exhaustive software/environment lock remain unavailable.
